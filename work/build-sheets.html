Build sheets that fit the builder

I am building a full suit of science-fiction armour: a foam version for Halloween and a plate version over the longer term. Printed costume patterns come in one size. I made the instructions a tool: enter eight measurements once, and every dimension, pattern and weight on the page, and the foam order, rescales to you.

How the page is built

The design problem

Build instructions usually separate into three documents: a pattern, a materials list and a guide. They disagree the moment you scale anything. Enlarge the pattern by ten percent and the materials list is wrong, and nothing tells you.

I wanted one page where that cannot happen, because every number downstream is calculated from the same few inputs. The interface work was making a page full of derived numbers feel trustworthy and readable to someone with a knife in one hand.

Key decisions

It opens already working

The page loads with a complete example and says so: the numbers are "an example for a 1780 mm (5 ft 10 in) wearer". Every diagram is drawn before you type anything. An empty form would show nothing about what the tool does.

One measurement is enough to start

A button estimates the remaining measurements from height. The estimates are rough and replaceable, and they turn an eight-field barrier into a one-field start. Precision can come later, field by field.

Say how to measure, not just what

The fit panel tells you to measure "over the clothes you will wear underneath" and that circumferences are "at the widest point". A measurement taken the wrong way is worse than none, because the page will faithfully scale the error.

Units and prices are honest about themselves

One switch changes the whole page between millimetres and inches. The shopping list gives each price as a low-to-high range, says which two prices were checked and on what date, and calls the rest estimates to confirm at checkout.

Instructions with an opinion

Where there is a choice, the page makes a recommendation in plain words. The simpler build tier is marked "This is the one to build". The size summary says "Check the span figure against your doorways". A reference lists options. A guide tells you which one.

Two builds, one part map

The foam suit and the plate suit share every part name and position. Learn the map once, and the Halloween build becomes a rehearsal for the long one.

Critique and next steps

  • It is one long page. Good for reading through once, slow for finding the gauntlet pattern on day twelve. It needs a way to pin the part being worked on.
  • Derived numbers do not show their source. When a plate width changes, nothing says which measurement moved it. Tapping a number should highlight its input.
  • It assumes a screen at the workbench. Patterns need a print layout at true scale, with a ruler mark to check the printer did not shrink it.
  • One builder so far. I wrote it and I use it. The real test is handing it to someone with different proportions and a different skill level and watching where they stop.
