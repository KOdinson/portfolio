Front Desk: a membership signup with nothing to find out later

I sold gym memberships and managed a club's front desk from 2016 to 2019. Gym pricing has a reputation, and it is earned by fees that appear after the signature. This is a signup page built on the opposite idea: show the whole first year before asking for a card.

Role
Sole designer and builder
Scope
Pricing page, content design, coded prototype
When
October 2026
Status

The prototype

The design problem

A membership has four prices and most signup pages show one. There is the monthly rate, a fee to join, a fee charged once a year, and a cost to leave. The headline number is the monthly rate, and the other three live in the agreement.

The business case for hiding them is a higher signup rate today. The case against is everything that happens in month three when the annual fee lands: the dispute, the front-desk argument, the cancellation, the review. I wanted to see what the page looks like when it is designed for month three.

That trade-off is my working assumption from selling memberships, not a measured result. I have no data comparing conversion or retention between the two approaches, and this page has not been tested with prospective members.

Key decisions

The receipt stays on screen

Choices are on the left. What they cost is on the right, pinned in place, and it updates with every tap. Nobody has to reach a review step to learn what they have built.

Prices by when they hit

The receipt has three rows, named for timing: today, each month after, once a year. That is how a person budgets. The fees' own names appear only in the explanation under each row.

One number to compare

"Your first 12 months, everything included" is the largest figure on the page and the only one highlighted. A $35 plan and a $49 plan cannot be compared by their monthly rates once fees differ. Their first-year totals can.

The page argues against itself

Under the total, the receipt says what the other plan would cost. If the cheaper option is the one you did not pick, it tells you, with the condition attached: "if you stay the full year".

Leaving is explained before joining

"How leaving works" sits directly above the payment button, in two or three sentences: how much notice, what it costs, whether you can pause. It changes with the plan.

The annual fee has a date

"First charged in your third month" is the sentence that prevents the argument. The fee is the same either way. Knowing when is the difference between a charge and a surprise.

Limits and next steps

  • Run it as a real experiment: this page against a monthly-rate-first page, measuring signups and also cancellations and disputes at 90 days. The second number is the one that decides it.
  • Five usability sessions asking one question after the task: "What will you be charged in March?" Count correct answers.
  • Design the staffed version. Most memberships are sold in person, so the same receipt needs to work on a tablet turned toward the customer.
  • Add tax and promotional pricing, which the prototype leaves out and which make honest totals harder.
