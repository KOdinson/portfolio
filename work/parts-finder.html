Parts Finder: start from the symptom, not the part number

A parts website assumes you already know what is broken. The person with a dishwasher full of water knows the symptom and nothing else. I fixed appliances for a year as a maintenance specialist. This search starts where the customer starts.

Role
Sole designer and builder
Scope

When

Status
Self-initiated concept. Invented models, parts and prices. Untested with customers.

The prototype

The appliance models, parts, prices and repair advice are invented for this prototype and should not be used to fix a real machine.

The design problem

Buying the wrong part is expensive for everyone. The customer waits a week, fits it, and the machine still does not work. The shop pays for a return. And often no part was needed at all: a blocked filter looks exactly like a failed pump from the outside.

A search box that takes a part number serves the person who has already diagnosed the fault. The harder and more common case is the person who has not.

The framing comes from my own repair work, not from research with parts customers or sellers.

Key decisions

Symptoms in the customer's words

"Water stays in the bottom", not "drain fault". The four options describe what a person can see or hear without opening anything.

The free fix is always step one

Every result list opens with something that costs nothing and takes minutes, in a green panel above the parts. A shop that tells you not to buy yet is a shop you come back to.

Three kinds of fit, said plainly

"Fits your DW-420", "Fits some 400-series. Check the part number on your old one", or "Does not fit". The middle state is the important one. Most sites round it up to yes.

Parts that do not fit are hidden, and countable

They are left out of the list by default, with a link saying how many were removed and why. The customer can see nothing is being concealed.

Effort is part of the listing

Each part shows the time and tools it takes to fit: "20 minutes · screwdriver". Price alone does not tell someone whether this is a job they can do.

Some repairs end with "call someone"

For a machine that will not power on, the page stops at the safe checks and says the rest means working near mains electricity. It names the conflict of interest too: "We sell the parts, and we would rather you did not fit them yourself."

The unknown model

Choosing "I do not know the model" does not block the search. Every part is still listed, each marked "Fit unknown until we have your model", and the page says where the label is. A dead end at the first field would lose the customers who most need help.

Limits and next steps

Ordering parts by "what to try first" implies knowledge of failure rates the prototype does not have. A real version would need repair data behind that ranking, and would need to say so when the data is thin. Repair advice also carries safety and liability weight that a concept can ignore and a product cannot.

  • Watch five people with a real broken appliance use it, and note the words they use for the symptom before they see mine.
  • Interview parts counter staff about the questions they ask to narrow a fault.
  • Measure whether leading with a free fix reduces returns enough to pay for the sales it delays.
  • Extend the symptom step to sounds and error codes, which are often the best clue.
