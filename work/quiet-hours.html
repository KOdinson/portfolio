Quiet Hours: settings that show their consequences

A notification settings page is usually a long column of switches. Each switch is easy to understand and the result of twenty of them is impossible to predict. This concept adds the one thing those pages leave out: how often your phone will buzz.

The prototype

The weekly volumes are sample figures for one invented account.

The design problem

Security monitoring teaches a lesson that applies to every app that sends notifications: when everything alerts, people stop reading alerts, and the important one is lost with the rest. A common way to fix a noisy app is to turn all of its notifications off, and then miss the one that mattered.

The settings page is where that could be prevented, and it gives people no way to see what they are choosing. "Activity on things you follow: on" does not say whether that means twice a week or thirty times.

Key decisions

One number for the whole page

The preview panel leads with interruptions per week and translates it to a daily rate. Every control on the left moves that number. People can tune to a figure they can live with and stop.

Each row carries its own volume

"About 31 a week" sits under the category name. The biggest source of noise is obvious before anything is changed, and it is often not the category people would have guessed.

Three choices, with a middle

Each category is Right away, Morning summary or Off. The middle option is the point: most notifications are worth knowing about and not worth being interrupted for.

Presets named by intent

"Everything, right away", "Only what needs me" and "Nearly silent" set every row at once. A preset stays highlighted only while the rows still match it, so a custom mix never pretends to be a preset.

One thing you cannot turn off

Security alerts have no control, and the row says why. A locked setting with no reason reads as the product overruling you. With the reason, it reads as protection.

Show what the summary will contain

The panel lists what would wait for the morning summary under the current settings. Choosing "summary" stops feeling like sending things into a void.

The preview uses a three-part bar with a legend and a count on every part, so the split between interrupting, waiting and not sent never depends on telling the colours apart.

Limits and next steps

The preview is only as good as the history behind it. A new account has no history, and a typical week is a poor guide to an unusual one. A real version would need a sensible starting estimate and a way to say "based on very little so far".

  • Test whether people set a different mix with the preview than without it, and whether they keep notifications on longer.
  • Ask what number feels acceptable. The design assumes people have one.
  • Design the feedback loop: a notification that turned out not to matter should offer "send these in the summary" on the spot.
  • Check the locked security row with users who feel strongly about control.
