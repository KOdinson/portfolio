Sheet: the system behind this portfolio

Thirteen case studies need to look like one body of work. This site runs on a small design system: six core colour tokens, three typefaces, and a handful of components borrowed from engineering drawings. This page is its documentation, rendered with the system itself.

The idea

My background is systems engineering, where every drawing carries a title block: a ruled grid in the corner saying what the sheet is, who drew it, its revision and its status. A reader checks that block before reading anything else.

A case study has the same need. Before a hiring manager reads, they want to know my role, the scope, the date, and whether the thing shipped. So each project is a "sheet" and opens with a title block. The rest of the system follows from that one borrowed object: ruled lines, monospace labels, squared corners, one accent colour.

Colour

Six tokens, each defined twice. The site follows the visitor's light or dark setting, and you are seeing one of the two sets now. Contrast ratios are measured against the page background, and every text colour clears the 4.5 to 1 minimum in both themes.

The orange is not the same orange in both themes. The one that reads on dark navy fails on pale grey, so the light theme uses a deeper burnt orange. The neutrals lean slightly blue so that grey text sits with the navy and does not look like a default.

Rules

Numbering means order

"Sheet 3 of 13" is true: there are thirteen and this is the third. Nothing else on the site is numbered for decoration.

Square corners, ruled lines

Layout components have square corners and no shadows. Hierarchy comes from two line weights: a heavy ink rule between major sections, a light rule within them.

Every case study ends the same way

A limits note, then next steps. The structure makes honesty the default and saves me deciding each time whether to mention what is untested.

Keyboard and motion

Every interactive element shows a 3-pixel accent focus ring. Smooth scrolling switches off for visitors who ask their system for reduced motion. Pages hold at 375 pixels wide with no sideways scroll.

What I'd change

  • The system lives in one stylesheet with no build step. That is right for ten pages and would not survive a team.
  • Uppercase condensed headlines are harder to read at length. Two of the project titles are long enough to feel it.
  • Specimen styles for individual case studies have crept into the shared stylesheet. They should move out.
  • There is no manual theme switch. The site trusts the system setting, which is correct most of the time and unhelpful when it is not.
