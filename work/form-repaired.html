Form, repaired: an accessibility before and after

A job application is a form people fill in under pressure, and a bad one quietly turns applicants away. I built a short application form the way they are often built, with eight common accessibility failures in it, then built it again with each one fixed. Both versions run side by side.

Role
Sole designer and builder
Scope
Accessibility review, form design, coded prototype
When
October 2026
Status
Self-initiated exercise. Built to WCAG 2.2 AA. Not yet tested with assistive-technology users.

The prototype

The "before" form is a composite I wrote to show common failures in one place. It is not a copy of any real organisation's form.

What the exercise shows

Accessible is also just better

None of the eight repairs helps only disabled users. Labels that stay put help anyone who looks away mid-field. Error messages that name the fix help everyone who gets one. The repaired form is easier on a phone, in sunlight, and when tired.

Most of it is writing

Five of the eight repairs are words: a label, a hint, an example, an error message, the word "optional". The "before" form asks "Date *". The repaired one asks "Date you can start" and shows what an answer looks like.

The button says what it does

"Submit" became "Send application". A small change that tells a screen-reader user tabbing through the page what the button is for without reading the form around it.

Errors are written as instructions

Each message starts with a verb and contains the answer's shape: "Enter an email address, like name@example.com". The word "invalid" does not appear. Nobody's start date is invalid. It is in a format the form did not explain.

Limits and next steps

Meeting the criteria is the floor. I built this against the WCAG 2.2 success criteria and measured the contrast values quoted here. I have not yet run it with screen-reader users or people using voice control or switch access, and that testing regularly finds problems a checklist misses.

  • Walk through both versions with NVDA and VoiceOver and write down exactly what is announced at each step.
  • Sessions with three to five people who use assistive technology daily.
  • Extend the repaired form to the hard parts of a real application: file upload, work history that repeats, and saving progress.
