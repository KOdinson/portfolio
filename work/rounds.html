Rounds: a patrol log for the night shift

In 2021 I was head security guard for a building in Denver. I ran sweeps, planned access control and trained new guards. This is a patrol log designed for the conditions of that job: walking, one hand free, bad light, and a report due at the end.

Role
Sole designer and builder
Scope
Flow design, interaction design, coded prototype
When
October 2026
Status
Self-initiated concept. Sample site. Untested with guards.

The prototype

Designing for the conditions

Most apps assume a person sitting still, looking at the screen, with two hands. A guard on a round is none of those. I wrote down the conditions first and let them make the decisions.

Walking
One glance, one tapThe screen answers "where am I in the round" with a segmented bar, and the usual case, all clear, is a single tap.
One hand
Everything at the bottomBoth actions sit in the lowest part of the screen at 62 pixels tall, inside the reach of a thumb. Nothing that matters is at the top.
Dark
A dark screen and a legible faceNear-black background so the phone does not light up a stairwell or wreck night vision. The typeface is Atkinson Hyperlegible, drawn for low-vision readers so similar letters stay distinct.
Tired
No typingAn issue is a type, one safety question and an optional photo. The keyboard never opens.
End of shift
The report writes itselfFinishing the round produces the handover note from the taps: counts, times, and each issue on its own line.

These conditions are my working assumptions from doing the job, not research findings. I have not interviewed or observed other guards for this project.

Key decisions

Each checkpoint says what "clear" means

Under "Loading dock" it reads "Roll door down, side door locked". A new guard does not have to remember what to look for, and two guards mean the same thing when they tap All clear.

The app knows where you are in the round

The footer names the current checkpoint and the two buttons apply to it. There is no selecting a row first. The list above is a record, and the buttons are the tool.

All clear is right, Issue is left

The common action sits under a right thumb. The two buttons differ in colour, in word and in position, and a checked point shows a tick while a flagged one shows an exclamation mark.

People before paperwork

Every issue asks "Does anyone need help right now?" A yes puts the supervisor's number on screen with the line "Call now, report after." The report can wait. The form says so.

Times are recorded, not entered

Each tap stamps the clock. The guard never types a time, and the log cannot drift from what happened.

The handover is plain text

The shift note is selectable text in a box, ready to paste into whatever the site already uses. A tool for guards has to fit beside the logbook, radio and email that are already there.

Limits and next steps

The prototype assumes a fixed route and trusts the guard's tap. A real system would need proof of presence, such as a tag at each checkpoint, and that changes the interaction. It also needs to work with no signal in a basement.

  • Walk a real round with it. Stairs, a flashlight in the other hand, gloves. Count mis-taps.
  • Interview five guards and two supervisors about what the current log is used for after it is written.
  • Test left-handed. The thumb-zone argument for button order flips.
  • Design the supervisor's morning view: last night's rounds across a site, issues first.
