Two Shopify themes: selling the job, not the part

Most of this portfolio is prototypes. These two are running Shopify stores. Each starts from Shopify's open-source Dawn theme and adds sections I wrote in Liquid, CSS and JavaScript, so the shop is organised around what the customer is trying to get done.

Role
Designer and builder
Scope
Five custom theme features across two stores, catalogue, artwork
When
October 2026
Status
Live on Shopify development stores. Invented products. Nothing is for sale and no shopper has tested them.

Forge and Foam

A supply shop for people building foam armour. A first-time builder does not want ten product pages. They want to know what to buy to get started, and to buy it in one go.

Fixit Supply

A dishwasher parts shop, and the real-store version of Parts Finder. The customer knows the symptom and not the part, so the home page asks what the machine is doing and offers the free fix before it offers anything to buy.

Both storefronts sit behind Shopify's development-store password page, so these are screenshots from the live themes. The password is available on request: get in touch.

Key decisions

A kit is one decision, so it is one request

The kit builder sends every ticked product to the cart in a single call and then opens the theme's own cart drawer. The shopper never visits a product page unless they want the detail, and the link to it is on every card.

The bar ends in a sentence

"You are $13.00 away from free shipping" and then "You have free shipping". The fill is a second signal. The words carry the meaning, so the bar works for someone who cannot see the colour.

The free fix comes before the paid one

Every symptom opens with a step that costs nothing. One symptom has no parts at all: it ends with a warning that the rest of the job needs a technician. A shop that says so is easier to trust on the parts it does sell.

"Fits" has three states

Fits your model, not confirmed, or pick a model first. "Not confirmed" tells the customer to check the old part number. Leaving the badge off would have looked like a yes.

The model is asked once

The model chosen on the home page is remembered in the browser and is already filled in on every product page.

The sticky bar is a second handle on the same form

Once the main button scrolls away, a bar at the bottom offers it again. It submits the theme's own product form, so errors and the cart behave the same from either button.

How it is built

Base
Shopify Dawn 16, which is open source. Everything listed below is added to it.
Sections
Build a kit, symptom finder, product fit, and sticky add to cart are theme sections with their own settings, so a merchant can place and edit them in the theme editor without touching code.
Cart bar
A snippet rendered inside the cart drawer and the cart page. Shopify re-renders both after every cart change, so the bar needs no JavaScript of its own. The threshold and both messages are theme settings.
Data
Fit, effort and "try first" are read from product metafields, with product tags as a fallback, so the catalogue can be loaded from a spreadsheet.
Apps
None. Everything runs in the theme.

Found by testing on the live store

The free-shipping bar showed its grey track and no fill. The cause was a rule in the base theme that hides every empty div, and the fill was an empty div. It is now a span, with a comment saying why.

The kit builder opened the cart drawer with nothing in it. The drawer was still marked as empty from the page load, and the theme hides its contents while that mark is there. The section now clears it before showing the new cart.

Neither bug showed up in the code check. Both showed up the first time I used the feature in the store.

Limits and next steps

These are development stores. The products, prices and models are invented, there is no stock, and checkout cannot take a real payment. The free-shipping bar is not yet matched by a shipping rate in the store settings, which a real shop would need before making the promise. I tested each feature myself in the theme editor's preview at desktop width, and the banners at one phone width. No shopper has used either store.

  • Watch someone with a real broken dishwasher use the symptom finder, and see whether the four symptoms are the ones they would choose.
  • Test every custom section on a phone and with a keyboard and screen reader.
  • Check the kit builder against products with variants and products that are sold out.
  • Measure what the sticky bar costs in page weight against what it is worth.
