What arrives first, and is something handling it?

A redesigned operator display for an autonomous counter-drone swarm. The interceptors decide what to chase. The operator supervises, and stops the system when it is wrong. This screen is built for that job.

Try the prototypeHow it works

Live prototype with simulated tracks. Select a threat and hold its intercept, or press and hold Abort.

Organised around a decision

Three questions, each with a fixed place on the screen.

What arrives first?A threat queue sorted by seconds to the perimeter, with the time as the largest number on each row.
Is something handling it?Each row states its pairing in a sentence: "W2 intercepting, 6 s to contact", or "Waiting for a free interceptor".
Do I need to step in?One control per threat to hold the intercept, and one guarded control to stop everything.
Shape first, colour second

Every state reads in greyscale.

Hostile tracks are diamonds and interceptors are circles. A held track or a recharging interceptor turns hollow. Colour confirms what the shape has already said.

Routine actions are one click. Abort takes a deliberate hold.

Hold to abort

A 1.2 second press, by pointer or by key. The button fills as you hold, so the delay explains itself.

Autonomy you can overrule

Holding a threat stops any interceptor engaging it. The log records it as "You put T3 on hold", so operator and system actions are never confused.

Simulation you cannot miss

A hazard-striped marker sits beside the title on every frame, where a live build would carry the opposite marker.
