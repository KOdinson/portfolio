The round and the report, with one thumb.

Rounds is a patrol log built for how a round really happens: walking, one hand free, bad light. Tap each checkpoint clear or flag it, and the shift handover writes itself.

Live prototype. Clear a few points, report an issue, finish the round.
Designed for the conditions

Most apps assume you are sitting down.

WalkingOne glance, one tapA segmented bar shows where you are in the round. "All clear" is a single tap.
One handEverything at the bottomBoth actions sit in thumb reach, 62 pixels tall. Nothing that matters is at the top.
DarkA screen that stays dimNear-black background, and a typeface drawn for low-vision readers.
TiredNo typingAn issue is a type, one safety question and an optional photo.
End of shift

The handover is already written.

Every tap stamps the clock. Finishing the round turns those taps into a plain-text shift note you can paste into whatever the site already uses: a logbook, an email, a radio call.

Nobody types a time, so the log cannot drift from what happened.

Shift note
Round finished 02:41 6 checkpoints, 5 clear, 1 with an issue. 02:14 Loading dock: Door unsecured (photo)
People before paperwork

One question comes before the report.

"Does anyone need help right now?"

Every issue asks it. A yes puts the supervisor's number on screen with the line "Call now, report after."

Clear means the same to everyone

Each checkpoint says what to look for: "Roll door down, side door locked". Two guards mean the same thing when they tap.

The app knows where you are

The footer names the current checkpoint and the buttons apply to it. No selecting a row first.
