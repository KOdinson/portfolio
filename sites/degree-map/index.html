The question every student opens a degree audit to ask:

"When do I graduate?"

Degree Map puts the answer at the top of the page, names the one course that controls it, and updates the date as you plan next term.

Try the prototypeHow it works

Live prototype with an invented program. Untick the Capstone Project and watch the date move.

The problem

A requirements list treats every course as equal. They are not.

Some courses run every term. Some run once a year. One of them is usually the real constraint on when you finish, and a checklist gives no sign which.

How it works

Answer first. Reasoning underneath.

The headline

You can finish in Fall 2027.

One course sets that date: Digital Forensics runs only in fall. Everything else fits before then.

A course you cannot take yet
SEC 250 Digital ForensicsRuns only in fall. The next chance is Fall 2027.

Locked courses stay on the page with their reason. Hiding one would hide the most important fact a student needs.

Plan and consequence together

Tick courses for next term and the finish date responds at once. Leave out a spring-only course and the page says what it cost you and how to get it back.

Conditions in plain words

"Open to you if you pass SEC 230 this term." The word prerequisite does not appear.

The question nobody mentions

The credit total says whether the plan is full-time or part-time, and prompts a check on financial aid before registration.

For advisors

The same audit, with the conversation started.

The full requirement list is still there, complete, in plain status words. A student who arrives knowing their date and their constraint can spend the appointment on the decision, not the decoding.
